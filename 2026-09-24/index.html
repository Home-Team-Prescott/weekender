⭐ This Week's Headliner
Prescott Highland Games & Celtic Faire
Sat Sep 26 & Sun Sep 27 · from 9:00 am both days • Watson Lake Park
Twenty-four years of it now. Caber toss, hammer throw and stone put, pipes and drums, Highland dancers, clan tents and merchants with handwoven tartans, under the granite at Watson Lake.
It draws between 4,500 and 6,000 people a year and it is run entirely by volunteers. Worth knowing before you go: advance online sales have closed, so buy at the gate. Tickets are available there both days.

This week's spotlight on
The foundry that casts Prescott's bronzes
The foundry that casts Prescott's bronzes
Most of us have walked past a bronze downtown without once wondering where it was actually made. A good number of them were made eight miles away, on 2nd Street in Prescott Valley.
Bronzesmith is a working foundry with a gallery attached, carrying work from more than forty artists, all of it cast on site by the lost-wax method. They run a tour of the foundry floor every Thursday at 11:00 am, $20 a person, and you do need to book ahead. The gallery itself is free and open Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 4:00.

🚗 Worth the Drive
Mingus Mountain, while the road is still open
About 45 minutes up Highway 89A
You have driven past the turnoff every time you went to Jerome. It is the one at the Summit Picnic Site, and it goes somewhere.
Forest Road 104 runs about two miles of pavement and then a half mile of gravel, which a normal car handles fine if you take that last stretch slowly. What is at the end is the rim, cooler air, and the Verde Valley dropping away underneath you.
The campground closes for the season on October 31, so this is the stretch of the year to go. Tent sites are $10 a night and sites with electric are $14, though the hookups have been out since a lightning strike and the Forest Service has not given a date for fixing them. Five vault toilets and drinking water on site. If you are only going up for an afternoon, none of that matters and the drive is the point.

📖 Worth the Read
The dress made of copper
A two-minute read
In 1925 Arizona had three electoral votes to deliver to Washington, and the person chosen to carry them was a woman from Prescott.
Sharlot Hall had already served as the territory's historian, the first woman to hold a salaried public office in Arizona. For the trip east the Arizona Industrial Congress commissioned her an overdress of copper links, with a matching bag and a hat trimmed in cactus, and she wore it to Calvin Coolidge's inauguration. The point was not the outfit. Arizona was a copper state, thirteen years into statehood and still being treated as frontier, and she walked into the capital wearing the argument.
She came home and spent the rest of her life on the other half of it. On June 20, 1927 she agreed to move her own collection of artifacts and documents into the 1864 Governor's Mansion, and on June 11, 1928 she opened it to the public as a museum.
It is still there, on West Gurley a few blocks off the Plaza. And this Saturday morning the grounds run Victorian Living History, which is on the list above, so you can go and stand in the place she built.
Source: Sharlot Hall Museum, “Who Was Sharlot Hall?”
