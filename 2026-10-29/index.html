🚗 Worth the Drive
Jerome, and a Friday about bats
About an hour, up 89A over Mingus Mountain
The old copper town hangs off Cleopatra Hill, and the best way to understand it is from the Douglas Mansion, built in 1916 just above the Little Daisy Mine. It is now Jerome State Historic Park, with a museum of photographs, artifacts and minerals, a 3-D model of the town and its mines underground, and a picnic area looking out over the Verde Valley.
Fitting for Halloween week: on Friday, October 30 at 11 a.m., the park hosts “All About Bats,” an outdoor talk with the Yavapai County Master Gardeners. Saturdays bring historical walking tours at 10 and 11 a.m., and Sundays a geology tour at 10:15, 11:15 and 12:15.
The park is open 9:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. daily. Entry is $10 for adults, $5 for ages 7 to 13, and free for kids 6 and under.

📖 Worth the Read
How Prescott became Arizona’s Christmas City
A two-minute read
Prescott’s Christmas story starts in 1865, inside the governor’s house, where Margaret McCormick set up the town’s first tree. Sharlot Hall later wrote about that frontier Christmas.
The tradition moved outdoors in 1916, while the courthouse on the plaza was still being built. Choirs sang and gifts went to the children. The courthouse itself was first strung with lights in the mid-1950s; two Days Past articles disagree on whether it was 1954 or 1955.
In 1962 Governor Paul Fannin named Prescott Arizona’s official “Christmas Town,” and in 1989 Governor Rose Mofford made it “Arizona’s Christmas City.” Next month, when the lights go up again, you will be standing in the middle of it.

Source: “Christmas on the Courthouse Plaza with Santa” by Elisabeth Ruffner and “Arizona Territory Frontier Christmas” by Parker Anderson, Days Past, Sharlot Hall Museum.
💡 Last week's answer
Before 1905, the town of Humboldt went by another name. What was it?
A. Val Verde
Humboldt was Val Verde, after the Val Verde smelter. On November 7, 1905 it was renamed for the German explorer Baron von Humboldt, who almost certainly never saw it. Agua Fria and Cherry Creek belong to its neighbor: Agua Fria was Dewey’s old name, and Cherry Creek was a name the post office turned down there in 1898.
Source: Town of Dewey-Humboldt, “Dewey-Humboldt History”
