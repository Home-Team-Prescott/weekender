This week's spotlight on
The Prescott Downtown Partnership
The people behind the Plaza
Merchants association since 1999 • Downtown Prescott
Every free concert you caught on the Plaza this summer, somebody put on. The Partnership is the shop owners, restaurants, and property owners of historic downtown, organized since 1999. They run the events, and they are the reason there are flower baskets hanging from the lampposts on Gurley. They are taking volunteers and board applications for 2026 if you have ever wanted a hand in how downtown runs.

🚗 Worth the Drive
Arcosanti, out past Cordes Junction
About 45 minutes down Highway 69
Most of us have passed the Cordes Junction exit a few hundred times without knowing there is an experimental town sitting just off it.
Arcosanti was started in 1970 as Paolo Soleri's argument that a city should be built up instead of out. Concrete apses, a bronze foundry that still pours, and those windbells you have probably seen hanging on a porch somewhere in Prescott without knowing where they came from.
Day visitors go through with a resident guide, Thursday through Sunday. Book ahead, some tours are by appointment only.

🕮 The Weekend Rabbit Hole
The bar that outran the fire
A two-minute read
On July 14, 1900, five city blocks of downtown Prescott burned. Whiskey Row went with them.
While it was burning, the regulars at the Palace did something very practical. They carried the bar out. The whole ornately carved 1880s Brunswick bar went across Montezuma Street to the courthouse plaza, along with as much liquor as anyone could get their arms around. Business reopened on the grass before the flames were even out.
The Palace was rebuilt for $50,000 and reopened in 1901. That same bar is still standing in there. You can lean on it Friday night.
Source: the Sharlot Hall Museum Days Past archive.
