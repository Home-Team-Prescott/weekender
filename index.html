🚗 Worth the Drive
Montezuma Castle, and Tuzigoot on the same ticket
Camp Verde and Clarkdale, about an hour from Prescott Valley
Montezuma Castle is a five-story cliff dwelling tucked into an alcove in a towering limestone cliff, and it is open every day from 9:00 am to 4:45 pm. The gate is a mile from the visitor center, so arrive with a little time to spare.
Here is the part most people miss: the $10 adult entrance covers Montezuma Castle and Tuzigoot National Monument in Clarkdale for 7 days. Children 15 and under are free. Montezuma Well, a separate part of the monument about 20 minutes away, is free and keeps the same hours.
All three close on Thanksgiving Day, November 26.

📖 Worth the Read
Gold on Lynx Creek, before there was a Prescott
A two-minute read
In the spring of 1863 a group of prospectors called the Walker Party, guided by Jack Swilling, followed the Hassayampa River into the mountains. They found some “color” on its headwaters, then real ore on Lynx Creek. That August, Surveyor General Clark rode out to see it and wrote in his journal, “The gold is very coarse & is found in almost every ravine.”
Word travelled fast. Arizona’s new territorial officials, headed for Tucson, were diverted to the diggings instead, and Governor John N. Goodwin decreed the capital would be there. In 1868 Lynx Creek saw Arizona’s first hydraulic mining. Years later one of the men working it was James K. Hall, father of Sharlot Hall, whose family settled on Lynx Creek near today’s Prescott Valley.
You can still try your luck. The Prescott National Forest runs the Lynx Creek Gold Pan Day Use Area for recreational gold panning. Check the forest’s current rules before you go.

💡 Last week's answer
Back in 1926, about how many miles of paved road did all of Arizona have?
A. Fewer than 2,000 miles
In 1926 Arizona had fewer than 2,000 miles of paved road. A century later it has 77,252. The whole country had only 140,735 paved miles back then. Every guess that came in last week was right.
Source: Stuart Rosebrook, “How One Woman’s Vision Kept All Roads Leading To Prescott,” Days Past, Sharlot Hall Museum
