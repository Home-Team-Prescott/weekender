A free walk through downtown history, before the season ends
A free walk through downtown history, before the season ends
Here is one most people who live here have never done. The Prescott Chamber of Commerce runs free 90-minute historical walking tours of downtown, most Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays at 10:00 am. The season runs May through November, so this weekend is one of the last chances until spring.
Tours meet at the Visitor Information Center on Goodwin Street at 9:55 am. Donations are appreciated, and the Chamber asks that you call ahead to confirm the tour is running. If family is in town for the holiday, it makes an easy morning before the parade.

🚗 Worth the Drive
Montezuma Castle, and the free walk at Montezuma Well
Camp Verde, under an hour from Prescott Valley
If the family is still in town Friday or the weekend, this is an easy half-day out: down I-17 to Camp Verde, where a five-story cliff dwelling sits in an alcove of a towering limestone cliff. The monument is open every day from 9:00 am to 4:45 pm, and the gate is a mile from the visitor center, so arrive with a little time to spare.
The $10 adult entrance covers Montezuma Castle and Tuzigoot National Monument in Clarkdale for 7 days, and children 15 and under are free. Montezuma Well, about 20 minutes away, is free and keeps the same hours.
Both are closed on Thanksgiving Day, November 26, so Friday is the first day to go.

📖 Worth the Read
The billion dollar camp on the side of a mountain
A two-minute read
Jerome started in 1876, when three prospectors staked claims on rich copper deposits. They sold to the group that formed the United Verde Copper Company in 1883, and the camp of board and canvas shacks was named for Eugene Jerome, the venture’s main backer.
By the early 20th century the United Verde was the largest producing copper mine in the Arizona Territory, and Jerome grew to nearly 15,000 people. Production peaked in 1929. The mining days ended in 1953, and for years afterward Jerome was virtually a ghost town.
That same year the Jerome Historical Society formed, and by 1956 it had a promise that no more buildings would be torn down in the main part of town. You can see the story for yourself at Jerome State Historic Park, in the Douglas Mansion above the old Little Daisy Mine.

💡 Last week's answer
Route 66 turns 100 this year. Back in the 1920s, one person spent a decade crossing the state to make sure the new highways ran through Prescott. Who was it?
B. Grace Sparkes of the Yavapai County Chamber
Grace Sparkes, secretary of the Yavapai County Chamber of Commerce, pushed for better roads and spent a decade traveling the state promoting them. Her work helped make Prescott the hub between U.S. 60 and U.S. 66. The Grace M. Sparkes Activity Center downtown carries her name.
