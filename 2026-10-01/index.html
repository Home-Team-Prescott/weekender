This week's spotlight on
The 4K planetarium on Willow Creek Road
The 4K planetarium on Willow Creek Road
Embry-Riddle has a full planetarium on its Prescott campus, and a lot of people who have lived here for years have never been inside it. It seats 116 and projects in 360 degrees at 4K.
It is not a campus-only thing. Public shows run most weekends, and this Saturday at 2:00 pm it is Superpower Dogs, ten dollars a seat. Building 76, the STEM building, at 3700 Willow Creek Road. Lots A and B are held for guests during shows, seats are booked online, and sold-out shows run a standby line.

🚗 Worth the Drive
Williams, and the 9:30 out of the depot
About an hour and a half north, up 89 to Ash Fork then east on I-40
Williams is the stretch of Route 66 that still looks like Route 66. Something over six blocks of historic buildings and shops, walkable end to end in an afternoon.
The Grand Canyon Railway leaves the 1908 depot at 233 N. Grand Canyon Blvd every morning at 9:30 and is back in Williams at 5:45. That is your whole day, and you never once go looking for parking at the South Rim.
If the train is more day than you want, Bearizona sits at 1500 E. Route 66 and is open seven days a week, 9:00 to 4:00. Most of it you see from the car.

📖 Worth the Read
The mayor on the horse
A two-minute read
There is a man on a horse in the middle of the Courthouse Plaza, and most people who walk past him every week could not tell you his name.
He is Buckey O'Neill, and he was the young mayor of Prescott when the Spanish-American War broke out in 1898. He led Arizona soldiers to Cuba and he fell on the battlefield there.
Nine years later the town put up the bronze. The sculptor was Solon H. Borglum, whose brother Gutzon would go on to carve Mount Rushmore. The Sharlot Hall Days Past account of the 1907 dedication records that Prescott business stopped for the day so that every citizen could attend, with Rough Rider veterans, Civil War veterans, military bands and civic leaders all turning out for it.
You can go stand at it this week without planning anything at all. That statue is the meeting point for the Prescott History Tour every morning at ten and the Ghost Tour every evening at seven, so there is nearly always somebody there.
Source: Sharlot Hall Museum Days Past, “Group to Repeat 1907 Dedication of Rough Rider Monument” by Warren Miller
