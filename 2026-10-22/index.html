A free 5K every Saturday morning
Every Saturday at 7:30 am, a group gathers on the Jan Alfano Trail at 3181 Willow Creek Road for parkrun, a free, timed 5K. You can walk it, jog it, run it, volunteer or just come and watch. It costs nothing, this week or ever.

🚗 Worth the Drive
The Verde Canyon by rail
The depot in Clarkdale, just down the mountain from Jerome
The Verde Canyon Railroad runs a four-hour round trip from its depot in Clarkdale to Perkinsville and back, along the Verde River through a canyon its own site says is revealed only by rail.
On the October schedule, the train leaves at 1:00 pm every day except Saturday, when there is a 9:30 am morning ride and a 3:30 pm Starlight Ride. Along the way it passes red-rock pinnacles and ancient ruins, crosses bridges and runs through a 734-foot tunnel cut through solid rock more than a century ago.
Get there early. The depot has a café, a gift store and a small museum. Fares change through the year, so check the rates before you go.

📖 Worth the Read
The pilot behind the airport’s name
A two-minute read
Ernest Alexander Love moved to Prescott at the age of three and grew up to be a hometown football star at Prescott High School, named an all-state guard. After graduating in 1914 he went to Stanford to study mechanical engineering.
When America entered World War I in 1917 he left school, enlisted and applied to fly. Of the fifteen young men in his group, he was the only one to qualify for pilot training. He reached France in 1918 and flew with the 147th Aero Squadron, on a squadron report that also lists Eddie Rickenbacker and Frank Luke. On September 15, 1918, he was shot down near Tronville, France. His parents did not learn of his death until February 1919. He was later reburied at Arlington National Cemetery.
Prescott named its airport for him: Ernest A. Love Field. On Saturday, when the gates open for AirFest, you will be standing on it.
Source: “Ernest Love: A Lot More Than Prescott’s Airport” by Alan Roesler and Michael Wurtz, and “Ernest Love, Prescott’s Hero of World War I” by Cindy Vaughn, Days Past, Sharlot Hall Museum.
💡 Last week's answer
In 1867 Arizona’s territorial capital left Prescott. Where did it go?
B. Tucson
The territorial capital moved south to Tucson in 1867 and came back to Prescott in 1877. It left for good in 1889, when it went to Phoenix.
Source: Sharlot Hall Museum Days Past, “America250: Tucson’s Long History” by Kristen Kauffman (1867 and 1877); Prescott Chamber of Commerce history page (1889)
