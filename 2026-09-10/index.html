💡 Spotlight
This week's spotlight on
Acker Park: the quiet overlook above downtown
Acker Park: the quiet overlook above downtown
Five minutes from the Courthouse Square is an 80-acre patch of native parkland that still feels tucked away. Acker Park has 3.24 miles of trails, native-plant signs, hilltop displays that identify the surrounding mountains, mosaic walls and a small amphitheater.
The one-mile loop is an easy first visit. Go late enough to catch the sunset toward Thumb Butte; parking is free, and summer hours run from 6:00 am to 10:00 pm through October 31.

🚗 Worth the Drive
Montezuma Castle and the sycamores of Beaver Creek
Camp Verde, about an hour from Prescott Valley
The short paved loop passes through a shaded sycamore grove to a five-story cliff dwelling set roughly 90 feet above the valley floor.
The visitor center and trail are open daily from 9:00 am to 4:45 pm. Give yourself about an hour, then make Camp Verde the lunch stop instead of the turnaround.

📖 Worth the Read
The day Prescott nearly lost the Plaza
A two-minute read
The Courthouse Plaza feels inevitable now, but in Prescott's first years it was anything but. Robert Groom's 1864 town survey left an unusually large public square at the center of town, and critics soon saw valuable building lots where others saw common ground.
A legal fight followed. Local merchants argued that the Plaza had been set aside for public use and that building over it would damage both the town and their businesses. The court agreed, preserving the square.
The first courthouse went up there in 1878. Trees, grass, walkways and a bandstand followed. For one stretch in 1880, the Plaza even grew watermelons for residents to enjoy on warm summer evenings.
The next time you cross the Square, it is worth remembering that Prescott's front porch survived because early residents decided a shared place mattered more than a few extra lots.
Source: Sharlot Hall Museum Days Past, “The Plaza: Prescott's Landmark Nearly Sold Off,” by Richard Gorby, edited by Kathy Krause.
