This week's spotlight on
The Hassayampa has live music every night
The Hassayampa has live music every night
Most people know the Hassayampa Inn as the grand old hotel on Gurley. Fewer know there is a musician playing from five to eight, seven nights a week, and it costs nothing to walk in and listen.
This week: Rick Cucuzza Thursday, Such A Ruckus Friday, Soft Winds Saturday, Double Up Monday.
It is the easiest good evening in Prescott, and it rarely turns up on an events calendar because it simply happens every day.

🚗 Worth the Drive
Jerome, hanging off Cleopatra Hill
About an hour, up 89A over Mingus Mountain
A copper town that once held thousands and now holds a few hundred, built on a grade steep enough that the old jail slid across the road and stayed there.
Go for the drive as much as the town. The climb over Mingus in monsoon season puts you level with weather you would normally watch from a porch, and the view back across the Verde Valley from the switchbacks is the whole argument for living up here in one picture.
Jerome State Historic Park, in the Douglas Mansion, is open 9:00 am to 4:30 pm daily.

📖 Worth the Read
The winter Doc Holliday stayed behind
A two-minute read
In early November 1879 a party of new arrivals reached Prescott. Wyatt Earp and his brother James lodged with Virgil, who was constable here. John Henry Holliday and Kate Elder took a boarding room in town.
Within weeks the Earps left for a silver camp down south called Tombstone. Doc did not go with them.
He was drifting out of dentistry and into full-time gambling, and Whiskey Row was the gambling centre of Arizona Territory. He also had tuberculosis, and the air up here was better than anything southern Arizona could offer. Prescott would become a treatment destination for TB patients within a generation. Whatever the reason, he stayed the winter, and came back again the following summer.
The last verified photograph ever taken of him was made here, at D. F. Mitchell's Capital Art Studio on North Cortez. You can stand on that street on Saturday.
D. F. Mitchell, Prescott, 1879-80. Signed "Yours truly, J. H. Holliday."
Source: True West Magazine, "Doc Holliday Before He Went to Tombstone", with research by Gary Roberts, Stuart Rosebrook, Tom Jonas and Mark Lee Gardner.
